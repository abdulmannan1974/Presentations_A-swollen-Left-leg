Job 2 · Name the trigger

Provoked or unprovoked? Start with the history.

Provoked

A recent transient trigger: major surgery, trauma or immobility. Hormones, pregnancy and puerperium also matter; strength and persistence of the trigger matter.

Unprovoked

No recent major clinical risk factor, and not on hormonal therapy. Review history, examination and baseline bloods. No extra cancer tests unless symptoms or signs point to one.

ChecklistThe Trigger rows on our DVT checklist capture exactly this, the same way for every patient.
Recurrence risk comes mainly from the clinical context, with bleeding risk and patient preference guiding duration—not from an inherited panel.
Teaching summary · BSH 2022 / NICE NG158

Definitions and cancer advice: NICE NG158 glossary, 1.8.1 and 1.8.2

[0:40] The clinical context is one of the most useful things we establish. Major surgery is not the same as a weak trigger. Pregnancy, the puerperium and hormonal therapy count as provoking circumstances in NICE terminology, but their implications differ. Persistent risks and previous events matter too. At three months, balance recurrence risk, bleeding and the patient’s preferences. The checklist makes the history consistent. Cancer investigation should follow symptoms, signs and the baseline assessment, rather than a reflex whole-body screen.

Job 3b · The lungs

PE: grade the risk, then match the treatment

High
Cardiac arrest, obstructive shock or persistent hypotension attributable to PE.
Unfractionated heparin infusion. Consider thrombolysis. Call senior and critical care.
Intermediate high
Stable, higher clinical risk, with RV dysfunction + raised troponin.
Anticoagulate and monitor closely. Rescue reperfusion if the patient deteriorates.
Intermediate low
Stable with one or neither marker at higher clinical risk; also low PESI with RV dysfunction / raised biomarkers.
Anticoagulate. Monitor and reassess; disposition follows clinical risk.
Low
Low clinical risk, without adverse RV / biomarker findings; assess outpatient suitability.
Anticoagulate. Consider outpatient treatment with a validated tool.
Routine primary thrombolysis is not recommended in intermediate or low risk PE. Thrombolysis is for the patient whose life is at stake.

ESC 2019 acute PE guideline, Tables 4 and 8 and recommendations. NICE NG158 1.2.1, 1.3.12, 1.6.2

[0:45] For PE, decide whether life is at risk. Haemodynamic instability means immediate senior and critical-care involvement, UFH and consideration of reperfusion. Stable patients with RV dysfunction and raised troponin at higher clinical risk need close monitoring; deterioration may trigger rescue reperfusion. A low PESI score alone does not make a patient low risk if RV dysfunction or biomarkers are abnormal. Outpatient treatment requires validated selection and a proper follow-up plan. Routine primary systemic thrombolysis is not for haemodynamically stable PE. This is the ESC risk framework, with NICE management advice, not a substitute for the full local PE pathway.

ESVS 2021 §2.3 · NICE NG158 §1.3–1.4 · Animation is conceptual, not a measured time course.

[0:55] Watch the first track: untreated, the clot grows. Click: anticoagulation suppresses further coagulation, reducing propagation and embolisation. It does not melt the thrombus. Click: endogenous fibrinolysis and remodelling do the clearing. That process is variable and can remain incomplete, so residual clot is not automatically treatment failure. New or worsening symptoms still deserve assessment. NICE recommends at least three months for proximal DVT or PE, with a planned review. Thrombolysis and mechanical clot removal are separate treatments for selected patients.

[1:05] The same patient can appear deficient, normal or even high, depending on when and how you test. Click through the columns. Acute illness, recent thrombosis and acquired disease can lower these proteins; recent clot especially affects S and antithrombin, whereas protein C may fall in DIC or severe sepsis. Heparin may lower antithrombin. Warfarin lowers both C and S. DOACs can falsely raise clot-based C/S activity and drug-matched antithrombin assays, hiding a deficiency. The method matters: chromogenic C and free S antigen avoid some direct drug effects. BSH says test these deficiencies, if indicated, after at least three months of treatment. Ongoing interference still needs an agreed plan; three months is not a washout.

[1:05] Lupus anticoagulant is a functional laboratory phenomenon: we time phospholipid-dependent clotting reactions. Click: the patient’s anticoagulant is in the tube as well. Click: it can produce false-positive or false-negative patterns, depending on drug, concentration and reagent. A normal routine PT or APTT does not reliably prove the sample is drug free. [1:05] Start from the clinical problem. A major transient trigger does not usually call for thrombophilia testing. Unprovoked VTE may justify an APS work-up because the drug choice can change; BSH also suggests this after minor triggers. NICE is narrower, focusing on a planned stop. Why the reflex is not harmless

A wrong result travels further than a right one

False label

"Protein S deficient"

Measured during the clot or on warfarin. Written in the notes, passed to the family.

acute clot / VKA → low PS → lifelong label

False APS

"Lupus anticoagulant positive"

Rivaroxaban or edoxaban in the sample. The patient is moved to warfarin for life, or a real APS is missed.

DOAC → false LA → wrong drug

False comfort

"Screen negative, stop at 3 months"

An unprovoked clot with a normal panel still carries the recurrence risk of an unprovoked clot.

normal panel ≠ low risk

Waste

"Please repeat in 3 months"

Early results are unreliable, so they are repeated. Cost, clinic time and worry for the patient.

test now → repeat later

Routine inherited results usually do not change the drug, intensity or monitoring of acute anticoagulation. A specific antithrombin question can be an exception.
Teaching summary · BSH thrombophilia testing guideline, 2022
[0:40] Four ways the reflex hurts: a false protein S deficiency label follows the family; a drug-affected LA becomes a false APS diagnosis, or true APS is missed; a normal panel gives false reassurance; and early tests need repeating. Click: routine inherited results usually do not change the drug, intensity or monitoring of acute treatment. A specific antithrombin or other urgent specialist question is different. Name the decision before requesting the test.
